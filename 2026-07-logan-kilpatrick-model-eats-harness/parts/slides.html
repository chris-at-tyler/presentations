Why the Model Eats the Harness

Logan Kilpatrick (Google AI Studio & the Gemini API) on agentic Gemini, Antigravity, vibe-coded games, Omni, and what's left for startups once the model absorbs the scaffolding.

51-min interview, Sequoia Capital YouTube, uploaded 2026-06-11 · recap by Chris
Source: youtube.com/watch?v=cMAs8z2dehs · auto-captions, so names/terms are lightly hedged

Recorded right after Google I/O. Logan runs AI Studio + the Gemini API — closest thing Google has to a developer-relations lead for Gemini. The whole interview orbits one idea: today's scaffolding (agent harnesses, tool-calling, RAG) is a scaffold BECAUSE the model can't do it natively yet — and that window keeps closing.
Agentic Gemini Era · 1:47

Antigravity: the new through-line

Before Gemini

Google's ~50-100 products had no shared thread — each shipped its own way of working.

Then: Gemini

The model itself became the through-line. Every product started routing through it.

Now: Antigravity

The same agent harness behind the IDE, CLI, and SDK is being rebased into Search, the Gemini app, Cloud, and Workspace.

"Coding is a specialized use case of the agent harness... it's proved to be the general-purpose agent harness." — 4:13

The API was the through-line for the Gemini era; the harness is the through-line for the agentic era. Antigravity isn't just a coding IDE — it's the engine getting dropped into every Google surface.

The Central Thesis · 38:41

"The model eats the harness"

1 · Bare weights2 years ago: tokens in, tokens out → 2 · Weights + external scaffoldingtool calling, search, code exec, containers — today's "alpha" → 3 · Model absorbs the scaffoldingit becomes native model behavior

"What we've historically thought of as the model is not the model anymore... the scaffolding is often a couple of steps ahead of what's baked into the model. Then the model eats that scaffolding." — 39:23

His prediction: today's harness-building alpha "perhaps won't be true, at least the way we think of it today, in 12 months." 40:03

This is the title of the talk and the spine of the whole interview — vibe-coded games, world models, Android apps in AI Studio are all instances of the same pattern: scaffolding proves a use case works, then the frontier model swallows it.
Counterpoint · 40:46

Then why do teams keep building their own harness?

Interviewer's pushback: companies build custom harnesses precisely to avoid lock-in to one provider's agent stack. Logan's answer: that's true today, less true as models generalize.

"You don't have a generalized model if it can't use another harness... we need something like harness-bench — actually measuring how good models are at adapting to different harnesses." — 41:28

His shrug on true out-of-distribution cases: "you're still going to be completely out of distribution even if you're using your own harness — so not sure it matters much."

A benchmark that doesn't exist yet, by his own admission — flag it as speculative, not something to go look up.
Scaffolding & Startup Edge · 38:32

If the model eats the harness, what's left to build?

  • 1Focus is the startup superpower. Big labs "have an obligation to do a bunch of things"; a focused team can run laps around them in one vertical — 42:51
  • 2Coding closed the gap on incumbents. Faster shipping erodes the moat of large, established codebases — 43:34
  • 3The agentic primitive is a new category to build on, risk tolerance included — teams willing to take more risk can win a cohort that wants that. 43:34

His honest caveat: "capability overhang" is a real threat, and there is a real question of whether general-purpose labs simply out-execute a given vertical. 42:09

24 months ago the fear was "less opportunity for startups." His read now: the opposite happened — "there's never been more opportunity to go build something." 41:48

World Models vs Engines · 28:01

Game engine + coding agent, not world model — yet

Near-term alpha for game creation is still coding agent + traditional game engine. World models are architecturally different and exciting, but in their current open-ended form aren't scaffolded for a reusable, replayable game loop.

"The definition of world models will blur." Historically a world model meant an action-conditioned video model — expensive, "very very not scalable" to run live. Omni is something else: understanding of the world, in a single model. — 30:15

Give this two minutes max — it's the conceptual bridge to the Omni slide, not a topic on its own.

Authentic Gen Media · 33:50

Omni edits the scene, not the person

"I don't use AI to make any of the content I produce — it's all my words, always my voice, always my image... What I like so much about Omni is that it's not changing me. It's changing a bunch of these other bits which are not me." — 33:47

His stated bar for gen-media taste: keep the words and the person real; let the model touch only the set, the background, the bits that aren't personhood.

Nice contrast to raise live: this is explicitly not the "AI avatar" vision — it's closer to "the same interview, better production value."

Wrap Up

If you only remember five things

  • The model eats the harness. Scaffolding proves a use case works; the frontier model absorbs it soon after — coding agents today, other domains next. 38:41
  • Google's agentic push is "crawl" by design, not by capability gap — stewardship of 13B+ user products, not a technology ceiling. 8:24
  • Omni is one model, not a router across five. First real use case shipped is video editing; it edits the scene, deliberately not the person. 31:10 & 33:47
  • Vibe-coded games: model quality isn't the gap, scaffolding is. His call — a stranger vibe-codes a genuinely fun game "this year." 27:07
  • Startups don't lose to "the model eats everything." Focus beats a general-purpose lab that's obligated to do everything at once. 42:51
